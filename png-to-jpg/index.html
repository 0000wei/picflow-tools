PNG to JPG Tips

PNG to JPG for Smaller File Size

The primary reason to convert PNG to JPG is reducing file size. PNG files use lossless compression that preserves every pixel, but this makes them 5 to 10 times larger than equivalent JPG files. For websites, large PNG images directly impact page load speed and bandwidth costs. By converting to JPG, you can reduce file sizes by 80% or more while maintaining visually identical quality for photographs and complex images. This size reduction translates to faster page loads, lower hosting costs, and better user experience — especially on mobile networks where data is limited.

Best Quality Settings for PNG to JPG

Finding the optimal JPG quality setting is key to getting the best results from your PNG conversion. For photographs and gradient-rich images, a quality setting of 85-90% is often indistinguishable from the original PNG while cutting file size dramatically. For screenshots and images with text, higher settings of 92-95% help preserve crisp edges and avoid visible compression artifacts around letters. Always preview your converted images at 100% zoom to check for quality issues. Remember that JPG does not support transparency — any transparent areas in your PNG will be filled with white by default, so plan accordingly.

When You Should Convert PNG to JPG

Convert PNG to JPG when file size is a priority and you do not need transparency. Common scenarios include uploading photographs to websites where load speed matters, sending images via email where attachment size limits apply, creating social media posts that need quick loading, and storing large photo collections where disk space is limited. Also convert to JPG when submitting images to platforms that compress images anyway — starting with a smaller JPG gives you more control over quality. However, keep originals as PNG for logos, graphics with text, screenshots, and any image that requires further editing, since JPG's lossy compression degrades quality with each re-save.
